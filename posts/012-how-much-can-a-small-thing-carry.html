Episode Twelve

How Much Can a Small Thing Carry?

A small grammar does not have to make the world small.

It can participate in a world whose depth comes from inheritance.

Take the old line again:

“Usual place. Seven.”

Three words. Almost no visible structure.

But what has already been completed for those words to work?

Which place counts as usual.

What seven measures.

Who is included.

Whether the plan is a proposal or a settlement.

None of that has to be restated every time.

Prior completions have become available as background conditions for a new one.

The present utterance inherits work.

This is one way finite forms participate in indefinite depth.

The local event can be small because earlier events have already done part of the work.

Mathematics does this relentlessly.

“By the theorem…”

The phrase can carry a proof that took pages to establish.

The new step does not contain those pages. It inherits their result.

A theorem name is not magic.

It is a handle onto completed structure.

The handle is short because the route behind it has already been made available somewhere else.

Language is full of this kind of compression.

“The usual.”

“Same as last time.”

“Approved.”

“As before.”

The danger is to imagine that the visible phrase contains all the intelligibility.

It does not.

The phrase participates in an inherited field that can be much larger than itself.

This is why a finite grammar need not be an inventory of all meanings.

The fifty-four do not need a special cell for friendship, geometry, cooking, grief, law, or music.

Those worlds can be deep because completed results condition later completions.

Local forms can recur inside larger structures.

A settled plan can become an input to a repair.

A repaired result can stand.

A standing result can be related to another.

The depth comes from composition, not from adding primitive dimensions forever.

Composition does not mean we already possess an algebra for every transition.

We should not pretend the sequence of coordinates is mechanically derivable.

The stronger and safer claim is simpler:

Completed intelligibilities can condition later completed intelligibilities.

That is enough to change the scale of the picture.

A short present event can carry a long past.

A finite local grammar can participate in structures whose practical depth has no obvious bound.

But inheritance does not only travel through time inside one mind.

A compressed result can pass from one person to another.

That is where the next pressure appears.

If I tell you “I already salted it,” you may not receive my exact tasting history.

You may still know enough not to add another spoonful.

What, exactly, has become shared?
