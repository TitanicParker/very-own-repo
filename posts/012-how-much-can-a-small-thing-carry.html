Episode Twelve

How Much Can a Small Thing Carry?

A small grammar does not have to make the world small.

Its local forms can participate in a world whose depth comes from inheritance.

Imagine two people setting out on bicycles.

One asks which way they are going.

The other says:

“Same route as yesterday.”

Four words can now carry miles.

The hill after the bridge. The turn by the school. The rough patch near the farm. The place they stopped for water. The way home.

None of that has to be spoken again for it to help organize what happens next.

The sentence does not contain the route in the way a box contains an object.

The route has already become available through earlier experience.

The short present expression can call that completed work forward.

This is one way finite local forms participate in indefinite depth.

The present event can be small because earlier events have already done part of the work.

A small thing can be deep by inheritance.

Mathematics does this relentlessly.

“By the theorem…”

The phrase can carry a proof that took pages to establish.

The new step does not contain those pages. It inherits their result.

A theorem name is not magic.

It is a handle onto completed structure.

The handle can be short because the route behind it has already been made available somewhere else.

So can a map legend, a family nickname, a familiar chord change, a workshop mark, a recipe instruction, a single raised eyebrow.

The visible present can be tiny while the field it activates is enormous.

Compression is not emptiness. It is inherited density.

This is why a finite grammar need not be an inventory of all meanings.

The fifty-four do not need a special cell for friendship, geometry, cooking, grief, law or music.

Those worlds can deepen because completed results condition later completions.

Local forms can recur inside larger structures.

A settled plan can become an input to a repair. A repaired result can stand. A standing result can be related to another.

The depth comes from what completed things can enter into next, not from adding primitive dimensions forever.

That does not mean we already possess an algebra for every transition.

We should not pretend the sequence of coordinates is mechanically derivable.

The stronger and safer claim is simpler:

Completed intelligibilities can condition later completed intelligibilities.

Now the scale changes.

A short present event can carry a long past.

A finite local grammar can participate in structures whose practical depth has no obvious bound.

And inheritance does not remain inside one mind.

One rider can say “same route as yesterday,” and another can take up the route too.

A compressed result can cross between people.

That is where the next pressure appears.

If I tell you, “I already salted it,” you do not receive my exact tasting history.

You may still know enough not to add another spoonful.

What, exactly, has become shared?
