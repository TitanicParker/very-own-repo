Episode Twenty-One

The Map Outgrew the Sentence

We began by looking at sentences.

Something in them seemed to settle.

A demand opened.

The sentence developed.

At some point, the whole became enough.

At first, naturally, the language of the theory followed the place where it had been found.

The architecture looked like an architecture of sentences.

Then the field widened.

Fairy tales.

Proof.

Objects.

Relations.

Continuation.

Translation.

The names changed.

The architecture did not.

Earlier names tried to say what the system seemed to be doing then.

Work became Transformation Pattern.

Orientation became Completion Topology.

Survivor became Persistence Mode.

This was not a change of furniture.

It was a change in our understanding of the room.

The structure stayed while the explanatory level widened.

Episode Eighteen separated completed form from formation history.

The theory has a formation history too.

Its route matters.

Its present structure is not reducible to that route.

We thought we had found something about sentences.

Sentences may have been where it first became visible.

That distinction matters.

A microscope does not become a theory of glass because glass helped us see through it.

A map does not belong only to the first road drawn upon it.

The question has changed.

Not:

What kinds of sentence endings are there?

But:

What kinds of completed intelligibility can a sentence reveal?

The map stayed still while our idea of the territory grew.

And now the obvious question is difficult to avoid.

If this is not merely a theory of sentences, whose subject is it?
