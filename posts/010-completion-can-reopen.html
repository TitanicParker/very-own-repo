Episode Ten

Completion Can Reopen

Completion was never supposed to mean that nothing could ever matter again.

Enough is local to a live demand.

Imagine two friends planning a walk.

They choose the old footbridge, Saturday morning, ten o'clock.

One replies:

“Perfect. See you there.”

The plan is not tentative anymore.

They know where, when and enough of what follows to stop arranging it.

The demand has genuinely settled.

Friday night, rain comes hard enough to lift the river.

By morning a barrier has been put across the path.

FOOTBRIDGE CLOSED.

Was yesterday's completion fake?

No.

The plan genuinely settled the demand that was live when it was made.

A new condition has entered the field.

The old plan can remain perfectly intelligible and still stop being enough for action.

The river has not travelled backward through time and made Friday's agreement incomplete.

It has changed what can continue from that agreement now.

So reopening is not retroactive incompletion.

It is the return of live unresolvedness where a prior result had once been enough.

The future can place pressure on an inherited completion.

Sometimes the pressure comes from the world.

A bridge closes. Weather changes. A part breaks. A person becomes unavailable.

Nothing about the earlier completion has to have been defective.

Sometimes the pressure comes from knowledge.

A fact you did not have becomes available.

The old completion may have been reasonable in the old field and inadequate in the new one.

Sometimes the pressure comes from a new demand.

“Where shall we meet?” may have been settled.

Later the question becomes, “Where can we meet without crossing the river?”

This lets us preserve something important.

We do not have to choose between saying the past was never complete and pretending the future has no right to disturb it.

A completion can be real and revisable.

Reopening is not a fourth Completion Topology beside Focal, Distributed and Release.

It is a later event in which unresolvedness becomes live again.

The primitive grammar has not changed. The field has.

And not every reopening is Repair.

Sometimes the earlier completion was faulty.

Sometimes the world changed after a perfectly good completion.

Those two histories can look almost identical from the surface.

That is where the next distinction begins.

“Thursday” can replace “Tuesday” because Tuesday was a mistake.

Or because Tuesday used to be right and then the meeting moved.

A correction is not always a rewind.
