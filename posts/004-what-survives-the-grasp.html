Chapter Five

What Survives the Grasp?

Chapter Three ended with eighteen broad locations already available to us.

Six Transformation Patterns. Three Completion Topologies.

But a completed intelligibility does not merely stop. It leaves something available.

Suppose I point to a ring of keys and say, “The blue one.”

Something has settled. One key now stands out from the others.

If I stop speaking, you are not left with nothing. You are left with a determinate thing you can now take as settled.

The achieved determination remains available as Standing.

Now suppose I say, “The blue one is for the back door.”

The key has not merely been identified. A bearing now travels with it.

Wherever that key enters the next part of the situation, it carries something with it: back door, not front door; this use, not every use.

The achieved result remains through a constitutive Bearing.

Now suppose I say, “The blue one opens the back door.”

Again, the key is clear. Again, the relation is clear. But something further has become available.

You can now do something you could not reliably do before the intelligibility settled.

The achieved result remains as Efficacy: an operative difference in what can happen.

These are not three kinds of subject matter.

Keys can appear in all three. So can laws, promises, maps, jokes, equations, instructions, memories and ordinary conversations.

We are asking what form the completed intelligibility leaves available to what comes next.

What survives the grasp?

The first Persistence Mode is the simplest to feel.

We call it Standing.

“That is Maria.”

“The door is locked.”

“This is the cause.”

Something now stands as settled enough to be taken as what is.

Standing does not mean eternal truth.

“The shop is closed” may stop being true tomorrow. “That is the winning move” may turn out to be mistaken. “This is the cause” may later be revised.

The Persistence Mode concerns how the achieved completion remains available.

It is a mode of availability, not a guarantee of permanence.

Nor does Standing require a sentence containing the word “is.”

Someone asks, “Which house?”

You point: “The yellow one.”

No copula appears. Yet the completed distinction leaves one house standing as the answer.

Grammar can express a Persistence Mode without naming it.

Standing is the achieved determination itself remaining available as the completed result.

Once “the yellow one” has landed, we can say “it,” “that house,” “the place,” or simply continue as though the selection were already in hand.

The grasp has created standing.

Something can now be carried forward as settled.

The second Persistence Mode is quieter because it often disappears into context.

We call it Bearing.

“Only if it rains.”

“For children under twelve.”

“Except on Sundays.”

The completion leaves a bearing that has to travel with whatever follows.

A condition is an obvious example.

“You can use the garden only if the gate is open.”

Once understood, the condition does not need to be restated every second. But if we continue lawfully, it remains with the permission.

The achieved result persists through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, source, proportion, frame, support, or mutual determination.

A relation can persist this way too.

“She is his sister.”

Later, someone says, “Ask her.”

The sister-relation may still matter even though it is not repeated. It can continue to shape what “her” means, why she is relevant, or what follows from asking.

Bearing is constitutive dependence.

Bearing is not merely background information.

Background can be forgotten without changing the grasp. A live bearing cannot.

If “except on Sundays” is part of the settled intelligibility, dropping it can change what is permitted.

What persists as Bearing remains what it is through that constitutive relation.

This is one place where ordinary fluency is almost magical.

We carry enormous numbers of these bearings without announcing them.

Who “she” is. Which Tuesday. Under what condition. Relative to what standard. With which exception. From whose point of view.

Understanding is full of achieved results that remain what they are through constitutive bearings.

The third Persistence Mode is often the easiest to notice in practical life.

We call it Efficacy.

“Press this button.”

“Now you can open the door.”

“That solves it.”

The achieved result remains as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.

Efficacy does not mean the sentence itself performs a physical action.

“The bridge is safe to cross” may leave crossing available.

“That proof is sufficient” may leave a theorem usable.

“We agree” may leave a decision actionable.

What persists is an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.

A recipe makes this very easy to feel.

“The onions are soft.”

That can leave the achieved determination as Standing.

“Keep the heat low.”

That can persist through Bearing.

“Now add the tomatoes.”

That can persist as Efficacy.

Again, these are not three moments in a fixed sequence.

An intelligibility does not move from Standing to Bearing to Efficacy as a sequence.

A completed grasp can persist in any one of these three modes.

Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy are coordinates, not stages.

Now return to the blue key.

“The blue one.”

“The blue one is for the back door.”

“The blue one opens the back door.”

The topic barely changes. The focus barely changes.

What changes is what the successful grasp leaves us with.

That means two completed intelligibilities can do the same Transformation Pattern, with the same Completion Topology, and still differ structurally.

The missing difference was not in the Transformation Pattern.

It was not in Focal, Distributed, or Release.

It was in the Persistence Mode.

The third dimension was already there.

Take Differentiation × Focal.

“Her.” One person stands as selected: Standing.

“Her, for the first group.” The selection carries a governing assignment: Bearing.

“Her—send her in.” The selection leaves an executable next move: Efficacy.

Same Transformation Pattern. Same Completion Topology. Different Persistence Mode.

Take Relating × Distributed.

“These two schedules match.” A correspondence stands: Standing.

“Keep the two schedules aligned.” A relation must continue to accompany the process: Bearing.

“Once they match, the transfer can proceed.” The relation leaves a consequence available: Efficacy.

The third coordinate is doing real work.

Take Repair × Release.

“That route is no longer the route.” A corrected standing remains: Standing.

“From now on, avoid the bridge.” The release carries a continuing constraint: Bearing.

“Turn back here.” The release leaves a corrective move available: Efficacy.

Release topology can persist as Standing, Bearing, or Efficacy.

We can now see why the earlier number eighteen was not enough.

Each of the six Transformation Patterns can complete through Focal, Distributed, or Release topology.

And each of those completed forms can persist as Standing, Bearing, or Efficacy.

The space has another dimension.

Six Transformation Patterns.

Three Completion Topologies.

Three Persistence Modes.

6 × 3 × 3 = 54.

This is the first moment when the number can be earned rather than announced.

Fifty-four is not a list of sentence types.

It is not a catalogue of meanings.

It is the finite combination of three questions every completed intelligibility appears to answer at once:

What Transformation Pattern was realized?

What Completion Topology made it sufficient?

How does it persist?

Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, Settlement.

Focal, Distributed, Release.

Standing, Bearing, Efficacy.

Not one after another.

One completed intelligibility, grasped in three dimensions at once.

Meaning remains as large as it ever was.

Nothing about this tells us what people must think, what a story must mean, what a theorem must prove, what a promise must matter to someone, or what a life must become.

The claim is narrower and stranger.

Whatever meaning becomes ours, it appears to become graspable through one of these fifty-four forms.

That is now a real claim.

And real claims have to survive contact with things that do not care whether the theory is elegant.

Ordinary sentences. Fairy tales. Instructions. Arguments. Mathematics.

So the next question is no longer what the 54 are.

Are they actually enough?

That is where the corpus becomes useful.

And that is where we go next.
