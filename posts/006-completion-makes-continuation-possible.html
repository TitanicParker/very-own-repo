Chapter Six

Completion Makes Continuation Possible

In the fairy tale, each completed moment changed what the next moment could be.

Now let us make the same phenomenon almost impossible to miss.

Put two twigs across each other on a bench.

Nothing sophisticated.

ABCD

Four angles appear around the crossing.

Call them A, B, C and D.

We are going to prove one tiny thing:

A = C

The opposite angles are equal.

But at the beginning, we do not yet have that.

We can see A. We can see C.

We might even suspect they are equal.

Suspicion is not yet an earned relation.

Look first at A and B.

Together they lie along one straight twig.

A straight angle is 180°.

A + B = 180°

Now available
A and B are tied to a settled total.

That little completion matters.

Before it, A and B were merely neighboring angles.

After it, their relation can be carried.

Something has become enough to use.

Now look at B and C.

They also lie along one straight twig.

So they complete in the same way.

B + C = 180°

Now available
B and C are tied to the same settled total.

And now something genuinely new becomes possible.

We have two completed relations:

A + B = 180°
B + C = 180°

Because both are equal to the same thing, they can now be equated with each other.

A + B = B + C

This line was not available at the beginning.

Nothing about merely seeing four angles licensed it.

Two earlier completions made this completion possible.

Now B appears on both sides.

The same quantity is being added to A and to C.

Remove that same B from both sides.

A = C

Now available
The opposite angles are equal.

We have travelled almost nowhere.

Two twigs. Four letters. 180 degrees.

And yet the final equality was unavailable at the opening and available at the end.

What changed was not the picture. What changed was what had been earned.

Watch the continuity again.

A + B = 180°

B + C = 180°

A + B = B + C

A = C

That is not merely a list of true sentences.

The order is load-bearing.

The third line inherits the first two.

The fourth line inherits the third.

Each completion changes the lawful space of the next move.

Try starting at the end.

A = C

It may be true.

But without the path, the equality has not been made available by this proof.

The result and the route are not the same thing.

This is the mathematical version of something the fairy tale already showed us.

The hen hears a rustle because a world has become available in which there is something to hear.

She can be caught because a world has become available in which the fox is present and acting.

What comes next inherits what has become complete.

But mathematics tightens the condition.

A story can surprise us.

A proof cannot simply announce a later relation and call the continuity lawful.

The next step must be available from what has already been earned.

This gives us a better way to think about continuity.

Continuity is not the absence of endings.

It is not one endless unresolved flow.

Continuity can be built from completions that leave something available.

That matters for learning.

If a learner never gets hold of the first relation, the second relation has less to attach to.

If both relations become graspable and remain available, comparison becomes possible.

Then cancellation becomes possible.

Then a new equality becomes possible.

Development often has this shape.

You become able to do something.

That ability remains available.

Something that previously had nowhere to land can now be taken up.

A completed capacity becomes part of the opening condition for another one.

Progress has the same dependence.

If nothing can be retained as achieved, every moment has to begin again.

There is no accumulated ground.

To progress is, in part, to inherit what no longer has to be reopened from nothing.

And understanding?

Understanding is not merely having more statements in your possession.

Something becomes intelligible enough that another relation can now be seen, tested, used or completed.

One grasp changes the availability of the next.

This is why the fifty-four matter here.

Every one of these steps completes in some determinate way.

Each achieved result persists in some determinate way.

And because it persists, the next step does not begin from an empty field.

The forms of completion are also forms of inheritance.

We do not need to stamp a cell number on every line to see the phenomenon.

We can recover the coordinate when we need it.

For now, something more basic is becoming visible.

Completed intelligibility is one of the ways a future becomes available to understanding.

The proof began with four angles.

It ended with an equality that had not yet been earned.

Nothing entered from nowhere.

Each completed relation altered what could lawfully follow.

Completion is not the opposite of continuation.

Completion is what makes continuation possible.

Now we can return to the fifty-four and ask more precisely how a completion takes the shape it does.
