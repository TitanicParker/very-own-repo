Chapter Four

The Whole Field

You know the ingredients now.

There are no more primitive pieces coming.

So we can let them meet.

One Transformation Pattern.

One Completion Topology.

One Persistence Mode.

Six by three by three. Fifty-four forms.

Not fifty-four new definitions. Fifty-four conjunctions of the small world you have already met.

Each cell below has a compact canonical name and a tiny companion utterance.

The name does not replace the coordinate. The utterance does not define the cell.

They are crystallizations: small ways of letting the form become palpable.

The coordinate generates the form. The name and utterance let you catch it.

We will take one Transformation Pattern at a time.

Within it: Focal, Distributed, Release.

Within each topology: Standing, Bearing, Efficacy.

Nine cells. Then the next transformation.

There they are.

Nothing has been withheld.

You have met the primitive vocabulary, and now you have seen every place that vocabulary can generate.

The names are handles. The companion utterances are crystallizations. The coordinates remain the structure.

You do not need to memorize this chapter.

You will meet these forms again in ordinary sentences, stories, proofs, corrections, decisions and explanations.

The point of seeing the whole field now is simpler.

You know how large the world is.

From here on, the book is not going to keep enlarging the vocabulary.

It is going to find out what this vocabulary can do.

First we open the testing field: fairy tales and mathematical traversals that were never written for the fifty-four. Then we watch completion make continuation possible under narrative and mathematical pressure.

You have the map now. The rest is traversal.
