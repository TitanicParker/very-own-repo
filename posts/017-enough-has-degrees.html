Episode Seventeen

Enough Has Degrees

A thing can become clear before everything about it is clear.

Imagine a face appearing through fog.

At first there is only movement.

Then perhaps a person.

Then someone familiar.

Recognition arrives before perfect detail does.

The fog has not vanished.

You cannot count the eyelashes.

You may not know the colour of the coat.

But one question has stopped being open.

You know who it is.

Or think of learning to ride a bicycle.

No bell rings at the exact instant when wobbling becomes riding.

Yet there comes a point when the child is no longer merely being held up.

Something has become enough.

This does not make completion vague.

It makes completion answer to a demand.

If the question is “Can she ride?”, there can be enough evidence before every future ride is guaranteed.

Episode Two said that enough is not everything.

Now we can say something more precise.

Enough can have a shape without having a knife-edge.

Some completions are tightly constrained.

A key either turns the lock or it does not.

Some permit a wider region.

A sentence may become understandable while several harmless details remain open.

The 54 do not need to specify every detail of meaning.

They constrain the form by which the situation becomes complete enough.

Finite form does not mean finite meaning.

That is how a small grammar can live inside an indefinite world.

It does not replace the world with fifty-four boxes.

It tells us something about the shapes by which the world becomes graspable enough to continue from.

A finite grammar can constrain an indefinite world without reducing it to fifty-four meanings.

And now another question appears.

If two different paths can arrive at something equally enough...

is the landing the same as the journey?
