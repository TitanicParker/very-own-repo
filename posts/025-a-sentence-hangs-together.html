Episode Twenty-Five

A Sentence Hangs Together

Episode Twenty-Four ended with a question.

What kind of environment must already exist for a voice like mine to become possible within it?

Do not begin with civilization.

Do not begin with consciousness.

Begin with one sentence.

The blue cup is beside the kettle.

It looks almost too ordinary to inspect.

Seven words. A cup. A kettle. A colour. A relation.

But the sentence is not an inventory.

Take the words apart.

The. Blue. Cup. Is. Beside. The. Kettle.

Every piece remains available.

The sentence does not.

Put them back.

Blue bears on cup. Beside requires something on each side of the relation. The first the helps make one cup the relevant cup. The second helps make one kettle recoverable as the kettle in play.

The words begin constraining one another.

Change one member.

The blue cup is beside a kettle.

The furniture of the world can stay almost the same.

But the field has changed. The kettle no longer has to arrive as one already determinate kettle.

Remove the article.

The blue cup is beside kettle.

You may still recover what is meant.

But ordinary English now makes you feel that something expected in the relation has not been supplied.

The missing piece has traction because the others were expecting it.

That word may be useful.

Traction.

The parts do not merely sit next to one another. They grip. What one can intelligibly do depends on what the others are doing.

Now return to the door.

The door closed.

The door is closed.

Five minutes later, both may be perfectly true.

The same door may be standing in the same position.

Yet one sentence presents a closing as something that happened. The other presents a closed condition as something that obtains.

A tiny relational change gives a different whole.

This does not mean tense mechanically assigns a coordinate.

The sentence is not the landing. We already know that.

It means something more basic:

the sentence itself has internal organization capable of carrying different completed intelligibilities.

We usually call a successful version of this coherent.

That word can sound vague.

Here it can be made smaller.

Coherence is the mutual constraint by which the parts become usable together as one graspable whole.

That does not make every sentence true.

A lie can cohere. A mistake can cohere. Episode Twenty-Four showed how dangerous that can be.

Coherence is not correctness. It is hanging together strongly enough to be taken up.

And now the sentence looks different.

Not a string with meaning poured over it.

Not a row of independent tokens waiting to be decoded one by one.

A temporary relational community.

Its members remain distinguishable.

But while the sentence is doing its work, their roles are not independent.

The whole is made by their bearing upon one another.

That gives us a very small claim.

A sentence hangs together.

And it hangs together relationally.

But a strange thing happens next.

The relational community can turn around and make one of its own members stand alone.

Perhaps relation can make an island.
