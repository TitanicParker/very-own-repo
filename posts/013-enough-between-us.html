Episode Thirteen

Enough Between Us

Shared understanding does not require two people to become the same mind.

It requires enough common availability for the continuation that matters.

You are cooking.

Your hand moves toward the salt.

Someone beside you says:

“I already salted it.”

You stop.

Perhaps you taste before deciding what to do next.

The other person has changed your continuation with one short sentence.

What have they actually transferred to you?

Not the exact feel of the spoon in their hand.

Not their whole memory of the dish.

Not their private taste experience.

Yet something can become shared enough.

The dish has already received salt.

That completed result can now govern what both of you do next.

This is not mind reading.

It is not proof that your inner experiences match.

It is a practical alignment of available intelligibility.

You can test part of that alignment by continuation.

If you both now act as though the dish is unsalted, the supposed sharing has failed somewhere.

If your actions become compatible, that is evidence that enough has crossed.

Compatible continuation is not identical consciousness.

It is evidence of relevant shared availability.

The distinction matters because otherwise we ask language to do an impossible thing before counting as successful.

Conversation works because people do not rebuild each other from scratch.

One person leaves a completion available.

Another takes it up and continues from it.

Understanding becomes visible in what continuation is now possible.

This is why a promise, a warning, a correction, a proof step, and a recipe instruction can all matter socially.

They reorganize what another person can intelligibly do next.

The grammar is still local.

The social depth comes from chains of uptake.

One completion becomes available to another person, then conditions another completion, which can be passed on again.

Writing extends the interval.

A sentence can wait for a reader who is absent.

The original speaker can be gone.

If enough of the completion remains recoverable, continuation can cross time as well as persons.

Now the risk becomes clearer.

A completion can be shared even when it is false.

A mistaken result can organize compatible continuation too.

Agreement is not truth.

So shared understanding gives us power without guaranteeing correctness.

That is not a reason to reject inheritance.

It is a reason to ask what should remain available when an inherited completion needs to be checked.

We cannot rebuild every route every time.

But blind inheritance is brittle.

There must be some middle possibility.

Enough to proceed, and enough to find a way back when proceeding stops working.
