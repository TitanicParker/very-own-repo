Episode Twenty-Seven

Whose Demand?

Episode Two gave us a phrase that has carried a great deal of weight.

A live demand.

Which cup?

Which route?

Can this equality be established?

Has the decision been made?

Something is not yet enough.

Then comes an apparently obvious question.

Whose demand is it?

Notice how much that question has already decided.

There is an owner here.

There is a demand over there.

Perhaps there is another person, object, task or world beyond that.

The axes have been drawn before the phenomenon is inspected.

Return to the cups.

Six cups are on the table.

Someone says:

Take the cup.

The problem is not that the universe lacks information in general.

The present field has failed to make one candidate determinate enough for the continuation.

Not every reaching movement is licensed yet.

Then:

The blue one.

A difference becomes sufficient.

The field changes.

Now a continuation that was previously underdetermined can proceed.

We can certainly describe this psychologically.

One person wanted to know which cup.

Another person answered.

That description may be completely true.

But the completed intelligibility does not require the demand to be a private object inside either person.

The unresolved relation itself constrained what could count as enough.

Mathematics makes the point cleaner.

Suppose a proof has an unresolved target.

A = C

Random true statements do not discharge it.

The target constrains the route.

Some moves are available. Some are irrelevant. Some are unlawful.

A human can work on the proof. A machine can work on the proof.

The constraint does not need a conscious owner in order to constrain.

We already have a name for the smallest version of this power.

Structural agency.

A relation makes a difference to what can coherently follow.

A live demand now begins to look less mysterious.

It is unresolved relational governance over what can count as sufficient continuation.

Before completion, that governance appears as pressure.

Something still has to become determinate, available, related, repaired, integrated, or settled.

After completion, structural agency does not disappear.

The achieved result can remain available and constrain what happens next.

Before: unresolved constraint. After: inherited constraint.

pressure → completion → inheritance

Not every structurally agentive relation is a live demand.

An established theorem can constrain a later proof without itself being unresolved.

A promise can constrain action after the promise has already been made.

And this protects Settlement from becoming a synonym for all completion.

Every completion answers some operative constraint of sufficiency.

But Settlement is the special case in which a live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, adjudication or continuation is itself discharged.

“The blue one” can answer a demand by Differentiation.

“I’m taking the second offer” can be Settlement when all that remained live was the unresolved choice itself.

The demand is present in both. What changes is not the same.

So the better question is not:

Whose demand?

It is:

What relation is presently governing what can count as enough?

That question does not erase people.

It does not erase intention.

It simply refuses to make independently constituted actors the unexplained starting furniture of intelligibility.

And now the fifty-four look different again.

If completed intelligibility is a relational field becoming sufficient, perhaps the map is not classifying things at all.

Perhaps it is describing the shapes in which relational traction can finish.
