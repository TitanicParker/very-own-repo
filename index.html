You understood that sentence.

You are probably not thinking about how.

You are thinking about what it means.

That is normal.

Meaning takes the foreground very quickly.

It is doing that again now.

You are not assembling these sentences piece by piece in your awareness.

They are simply becoming clear enough to follow.

Some become clear almost at once. Some make you hold something open for a moment. Some make a difference matter. Some connect one thing to another. Some correct what you had started to think. Some gather several things into one. Some simply let the asking stop.

You already know all of this.

Not as a theory.

As something you do.

You do it while reading. You do it while listening. You do it while speaking. You do it while thinking.

You have been doing it for as long as you have been understanding anything.

Most of the time, the meaning is so interesting that the way it became graspable disappears behind it.

This book is about that disappearing part.

Not what things mean. The small forms by which they become clear enough to mean anything to you at all.

There do not appear to be very many of them.

We found fifty-four.

You already use them.

You just did.
