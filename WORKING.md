# Working Draft

Welcome.

You may notice quite early that this book is unusually clear. That is not an accident, and it is not simply a matter of style. The book is using the thing it is trying to show you.

You are reading for meaning. Of course you are. Meaning is compelling, and almost everything about reading trains your attention toward it. But this book is written to intelligibility: to the part of you that must be able to grasp what is being said before meaning can do anything at all.

So we will not ask you to carry sentences whose intelligibility we have not settled ourselves. Nothing is here merely because it sounds right. Every sentence has work to do.

That matters because this book is about something so natural that it is almost invisible: the small structures through which meaning becomes clear enough to be yours.

You already know them.

You have been using them all your life.

# Chapter One — Meaning Is Not Everything

Something has already happened between us. You read that sentence and understood it. You did not have to stop halfway through and ask yourself what kind of mental operation would allow the words to become intelligible. You did not inspect the sentence, identify the particular shape of understanding it required, perform that shape deliberately, and then collect the meaning at the end. You simply read it. The meaning was there for you, almost immediately, and whatever had to happen for it to be there happened so naturally that it did not present itself as a separate event at all. The same thing is happening now. And now. You are not merely looking at words. You are continuously realizing intelligibilities, one giving way to another, carrying enough of what has already become clear for the next thing to become clear in turn. We have several ordinary names for how successful this feels. Fluency is one. Clarity is another. Neither tells us very much about how extraordinary it is.

The strange thing about language is not that it sometimes works. The strange thing is how relentlessly it works. We speak while walking, driving, washing dishes, looking for keys, watching somebody’s face, thinking about what we are going to say next. We read messages while standing in queues. We listen to people who hesitate, interrupt themselves, change direction, choose the wrong word and correct it. We understand accents we have never heard before, sentences we have never encountered before, ideas that have never previously existed in exactly that form. Most of the time we do not congratulate ourselves on any of this. We do not even notice it. Meaning simply keeps arriving. The success is so complete that the mechanism of success has become transparent.

This book is about that transparency.

You are reading for meaning. That is what readers do, and this book has no intention of competing with it. Meaning is far too compelling. If I tell you that somebody left a bag on a train and came back three hours later to find it still on the seat, you are already somewhere inside the story. You may wonder what was in the bag, why nobody took it, whether the person was lucky, whether you would have gone back for it. Your own memories and expectations begin working immediately. The sentence opens onto a world, and the world is more interesting than the sentence. This is exactly as it should be. But there is something else occurring so successfully that your attention passes straight through it. Before any of those personal realizations can matter to you, what I have written must become intelligible enough for you to have them.

That is the peculiar position from which this book is written. You are reading for meaning. I am writing to intelligibility. I cannot decide what the words will mean in the full life of another person. I do not have your memories, temperament, history, private associations or present concerns. I cannot control what a sentence will illuminate when it reaches you. Nor would I want to. What I can do is make sure that I am not asking you to carry an intelligibility that I have not settled myself. Every sentence can know why it is here. Every paragraph can know what it is asking you to grasp. The meaning remains free to become yours.

That distinction will eventually matter enormously. A finite structure of intelligibility does not imply a finite world of meaning. It implies almost the opposite. Think of light passing through a prism. The prism does not invent everything illuminated by the light, and it does not decide what anybody will see in the colours. It provides a structure through which variation can appear. Human beings bring astonishing differences to understanding. Two people can hear the same sentence, understand it perfectly, and realize very different things through it. One remembers a childhood room. Another sees a practical consequence. Another hears an insult. Another recognizes a joke. The shared intelligibility has not erased their individuality. It has given their individuality something clear enough to work with.

For most of our lives there is no reason to separate these things. Meaning and the grasp of meaning arrive together so successfully that the distinction would be useless in ordinary conversation. If I tell you to put the blue cup beside the kettle, you do not need a theory of intelligibility. You need to know which cup I mean and where to put it. If somebody says, “I didn’t mean Tuesday, I meant Thursday,” you do not need to know what structural form has just been repaired. You correct your understanding and continue. Human beings were exquisitely fluent long before anybody needed the vocabulary in this book. We still do not need it. You can finish the book, forget every term in it, and understand people just as well tomorrow morning.

That is part of what makes this worth looking at.

There are many things we study because they are difficult. This is difficult to see for almost the opposite reason. We are too good at it. The machinery is hidden by success. Meaning does not merely occupy our attention; it rewards it. Understanding something is satisfying. A question becomes clear, a distinction suddenly matters, two things fall into relation, a mistaken arrangement is corrected, scattered parts become one thing, a lingering uncertainty finally settles. We experience the satisfaction of the intelligibility itself. We do not ordinarily turn around inside that satisfaction and ask what shape it had.

But we can.

Take the smallest possible example. Someone points into a crowded photograph and says, “Her.” Nothing grand has happened. One face among many has become the face that matters. Or imagine someone saying, “This one, not that one.” The selected thing now makes sense partly through the alternative it excludes. Or consider the words, “Only if it rains.” Whatever follows is not simply floating free; its intelligibility now carries a condition. Or, “That explains everything.” Earlier things that were already present acquire a new bearing together. These are ordinary moments. We pass through them constantly. Yet they do not satisfy us in the same way. The grasp has different shapes.

Notice that we do not have to stand outside the experience and divide it into artificial stages to see this. A sentence takes time, certainly. Words arrive in an order. Expectations can form, change and narrow. Later wording can determine how earlier wording is understood. All of that is real and useful to examine. But the structure we are interested in belongs inside the realized intelligibility itself. When a distinction becomes clear, its previous insufficiency and its achieved clarity belong to one articulated grasp. When a relation settles, the terms and the bearing that makes them intelligible together are not separate events glued together by an observer. We are describing the internal shape of something that has become enough.

Enough is an important word here.

Intelligibility does not require the end of the world. A sentence can be complete and still belong to a paragraph. A paragraph can be complete and still belong to an argument. A decision can settle one question and create another. A repaired bicycle can break again next week. A destination can be reached and immediately become the starting point of another journey. Completion, in the sense that matters here, is not absolute finality. It is the satisfaction of the particular demand that makes the present intelligibility what it is. Something has become clear enough to be taken, carried, related, acted upon, remembered, questioned, contradicted or continued.

We are constantly being satisfied in this way.

That may be the simplest reason to care about what follows. We spend our lives consuming meaning without ever needing to look at the form of the bite. We read novels, instructions, menus, legal agreements, love letters, jokes, equations, headlines and text messages. We listen to arguments and apologies. We tell stories. We give directions. We mutter to ourselves. We explain things to children. We lie. We misunderstand and recover. We make promises. We ask whether there is milk in the fridge. Across all of this extraordinary variety, intelligibility keeps finding ways to become enough.

The claim of this book is that those ways are not limitless.

Meaning may be. The things that can be thought, noticed, imagined, remembered, invented, desired, feared, discovered and communicated appear inexhaustible. But the forms by which an intelligibility becomes graspable are astonishingly small in number. Under the immense variety of meaning there is a tiny recurrent grammar. It does not tell us what to think. It does not tell us what anything means. It does not reduce one person’s realization to another’s. It tells us something much more modest and, once seen, much stranger: the ways in which meaning can become enough for us have a structure.

Later in this book I am going to ask you to entertain a claim that initially sounds almost absurd in its simplicity. An intelligibility can only do one of six Transformation Patterns. The way that work becomes enough has only three Completion Topologies. And what is realized through it can remain available in only three Persistence Modes. Six by three by three. Fifty-four.

The immediate response should probably be disbelief of a particular sort: not “that sounds impossibly complicated,” but “surely somebody would have noticed.”

Exactly.

If a sentence can ultimately only distinguish something, open something, relate something, correct something, integrate something or allow something to rest, surely that should have been obvious. If the achieved intelligibility can only stand as something, hold through some constitutive bearing, or remain as something that makes a difference in what can happen, surely somebody should have written that down a very long time ago. If these forms are present whenever we read, listen, speak and write, how could they possibly have remained unseen?

The answer may be embarrassingly simple.

Nobody needed to see them.

A child does not need a theory of balance to learn not to fall over. You do not need to know the anatomy of your hand in order to pick up a glass. And nobody needs a table of completed intelligibilities to understand “Pass me the salt.” The competence is prior to its description. The thing works whether or not anybody has named its workings. In fact, the better it works, the less reason there is to interrupt it with inspection.

That is why breakdown can be revealing, though breakdown is not the purpose of the book. Very occasionally a sentence refuses to settle. We reread it. We know all the words but cannot quite determine what the whole is asking us to take. Something points in two directions. A relation remains uncertain. A pronoun refuses to find its home. A qualification changes the apparent scope of something we thought we had already understood. Suddenly we become aware of labour that is usually invisible. We say that the writing is awkward, ambiguous or unclear. Then, when the problem is fixed, the labour disappears again. Meaning resumes its place in the foreground.

Fluency is what invisibility feels like when the structure is working.

Clarity is what successful intelligibility feels like from inside the grasp.

Neither requires us to know the mechanism. But now that the mechanism can be described, there is a pleasure in simply seeing it. There is a world here. Tiny distinctions we have made millions of times without naming them. Different ways a possibility can open. Different ways a relation can hold. Different ways a correction can become sufficient. Different ways parts can acquire a whole. Different kinds of rest. Different ways the achieved thing remains available inside everything that follows.

And because these forms are nearly contentless, they can participate in almost anything. A mathematical proof and an argument between friends can share a structural movement without sharing a subject. A bridge, a sentence and a promise can each instantiate a kind of bearing while being utterly different things. A pointing finger and a word on a page can perform the same kind of distinction. The structure does not make them secretly identical. It tells us the particular way in which each has become intelligible enough to be taken.

This is why the system should not feel like a cage around meaning. It is closer to a set of tiny hinges. There are not many ways for a hinge to hinge, but there is no obvious limit to the doors that can be built around one. The finite part is not the world. The finite part is not personality. It is not imagination. It is not culture or language or thought. The finite part is the little shape of satisfaction through which something becomes clear enough to enter all of those things.

There is one final fact worth knowing at the beginning, because it tells you something about where this book came from.

You will not learn this by asking a GPT to explain it to you.

A GPT is very good at meaning. That is part of the problem. Give a language model a sentence and ask what it means, and meaning rushes forward. Ask what the sentence is doing structurally and meaning tends to rush forward again. The same compelling success that hides these forms from ordinary human attention hides them surprisingly well inside systems built to handle language.

This book emerged through ten months of trying to make that distinction clear enough to teach.

The process eventually had to be broken apart. One GPT was asked to look at a sentence without knowing the grammar and describe what the sentence itself was doing. Another was asked to take that description and recover the structural coordinate. A third was given the coordinate without the sentence and asked to derive the intelligible form in the opposite direction. The point was not to make three machines agree. The separation was necessary because the thing we were trying to see kept disappearing into meaning.

That story belongs at the end of the book.

At the beginning, you only need the promise it gives us.

A GPT does not know this already.

Neither did you.

But you already know how to do it.

By the time we reach the final chapter, you will know the difference. And if this book has done its job properly, you will be able to open a fresh conversation, explain the grammar to a system that has never seen the fifty-four forms, and watch it begin to find them.

For now, there is only one thing I want you to notice.

You are still reading.

Meaning is still arriving.

And underneath it, almost perfectly invisible, something keeps becoming enough.

# How to Explain This to a GPT

There is a slightly ridiculous way to end this book.

Explain it to a GPT.

Not because a GPT is an authority on intelligibility. It is not. Not because a machine agreeing with us would make any of this true. It would not.

The reason is much simpler.

If we have spent this whole book trying to make something extraordinarily familiar explicit, then by the end we should be able to say what it is clearly enough that another system can do something with the explanation.

So let us try.

There is also a nice reversal here.

You have been reading this book for meaning. The book has been written to your intelligibility. For most of the journey, those two things have been allowed to work together without much fuss.

Now we are going to take the machinery out and put it on the table.

We are going to explain the grammar without giving away the fifty-four answers.

Then we are going to ask a GPT:

**What follows from this?**

That distinction matters.

We do not want to give it the table and ask it to repeat the table.

We do not want to tell it that a particular coordinate is called *Focal Mark*, then congratulate ourselves when it tells us that a focal mark is focal.

We do not want to give it the little companion utterance *This, not that* and ask it what kind of distinction might be involved.

That would be memory dressed up as derivation.

The interesting question is whether the forms can be recovered from the grammar itself.

So the GPT gets very little.

It gets six kinds of work.

It gets three ways a completion can take shape.

It gets three ways the achieved intelligibility can remain available.

And it gets one very important instruction:

**Do not add those three things together.**

That last part turns out to matter a great deal.

---

## The temptation to build the wrong machine

When people first see the structure, it is natural to read a coordinate as three descriptions placed beside one another.

Take a coordinate such as:

**Differentiation × Focal × Standing**

The temptation is to say:

Differentiation means a previously unavailable or non-operative difference becomes determinate enough to matter.

Focal means completion gathers around one privileged locus, and that locus is constitutive of sufficiency.

Standing means the achieved determination itself remains available as the completed result.

Therefore the answer must be something like:

> One distinct thing remains.

That is not terrible.

It is also not the system.

It treats the coordinate as though three independent properties have been attached to an object.

But the three dimensions are not three stickers.

They determine one another.

There is no completed Differentiation sitting there waiting for Focal to select one of its results.

There is no Focal operation that occurs first and then gets preserved by Standing.

There are not three little mechanisms working in sequence.

There is one intelligible form.

The distinction is toward a privileged locus in the particular way required for this completion to count as a distinction at all, and the standing is the way that very distinction exists as achieved.

The three dimensions meet inside one event.

This is what we have called **Dimensional Conjunction**.

It is not another dimension.

It is the rule that tells us how to read the dimensions we already have.

The coordinate is a constraint that generates a form.

It is not a sentence template.

This is the hardest thing to explain to the GPT, because GPTs are very good at combining descriptions.

Give one a red thing, a large thing and a wooden thing and it will happily give you a large red wooden thing.

That is exactly what we do not want here.

We want the conjunction to produce something more specific than the three definitions considered separately.

So before we ask the GPT for any of the fifty-four forms, we teach it the grammar.

## The six kinds of work

The first dimension asks what kind of unresolvedness is being answered.

There are six.

### Differentiation

A difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated or unable to matter becomes determinate enough to matter.

Something can now be told apart.

### Opening

Availability, reach, continuation, access or possibility becomes greater than it was.

Something can now open, continue, reach or become available where it could not before.

### Relating

A bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, reciprocity or mutual determination becomes constitutive of the situation.

Something now holds through how it bears upon something else.

### Repair

An organization fails relative to some operative criterion and is changed so that the misfit or fault no longer governs the result.

Something is set right.

### Integration

Separate parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole and thereby gain structure, mutual support or joint capacity.

The parts now belong together in a way they did not before.

### Settlement

A live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, completion, adjudication or continuation is discharged.

Something no longer asks to be carried further.

Those are the six families of work.

Not six subjects.

Not six moods.

Not six kinds of sentence.

Six things intelligibility can be doing.

---

## The three shapes of completion

Now we ask a different question.

What makes that work complete?

There are three possibilities.

### Focal

Completion gathers around one privileged locus.

There is something around which the intelligibility lands: an item, a source, a target, a pivot, a whole, a choice, a settlement point.

That privileged locus is not incidental. It is why this completion counts as complete.

### Distributed

Completion requires a constitutive plurality, span, sequence, range, reciprocal field, set of cases or system.

No single locus is sufficient by itself.

The completion exists across what is being held together.

### Release

Something that previously governed the situation loses its determining force.

This does not simply mean that something disappears.

It does not mean that the sentence contains a negative word.

It does not mean physical movement away.

It means that something which had been governing the intelligibility no longer governs it.

The completion occurs in that release.

---

## The three ways the result remains

There is one more question.

Once the intelligibility is achieved, what kind of achieved thing is it?

Again there are three possibilities.

### Standing

The achieved determination itself is what remains available.

Something now simply stands.

### Bearing

The achieved result remains what it is through some constitutive bearing: a dependency, contrast, condition, source, proportion, frame, support or mutual determination.

Take away the bearing and you no longer have the same completed intelligibility.

### Efficacy

The achieved result remains as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function or consequential difference in what can happen.

The completion has become effective.

Six by three by three.

Fifty-four.

But we are still not going to show the GPT the fifty-four.

That would spoil the game.

---

## One event, not three properties

Now we give the GPT the rule that matters most.

A coordinate such as

**Relation × Across × With**

does not mean:

a relation  
plus some plurality  
plus another relation that keeps it going.

It names one form in which Relation is possible only across a constitutive plurality and remains what it is through mutual bearing.

The dimensions constrain one another.

Change one of them and the identity of the completion should change.

That gives us a useful test.

Suppose the GPT produces a description for:

**Correction × Away × Does**

and we could change Away to Across without really changing the description.

Then the answer is too loose.

Or suppose the GPT describes a thing that is already fully corrected and then says that, because the survivor mode is Does, the corrected thing can now be useful.

That is also wrong.

Does is not a little usefulness operator attached at the end.

The efficacy has to belong to what this particular correction is.

Likewise, Away does not perform an operation on Correction.

It does not mean “do a correction, then release something.”

The release is the orientation of that correction.

The correction is complete *as* a de-governance.

This is subtle when explained abstractly.

In actual intelligibility, we do it without effort.

That has been the joke of the whole book.

---

## What the GPT is not allowed to know

Now we make the experiment fair.

The GPT may know the grammar.

It may know all six kinds of work.

It may know Toward, Across and Away.

It may know Is, With and Does.

It may know the rule of Dimensional Conjunction.

It may even silently consider neighbouring coordinates so that it does not produce something vague enough to fit several places.

But it does **not** get the answers.

It does not get the cell number.

It does not get the canonical name.

It does not get the little companion utterance.

It does not get a source sentence that has already been classified.

It does not get the table at the back of this book.

It gets only a coordinate.

Then we ask it to derive the form.

That is GPT-D.

D stands for **Derivable Understanding**.

The name is slightly grander than the job.

The job is simple:

> Given this position in the grammar, what intelligible form does it generate?

And we ask for the answer in ordinary language.

Not a proof.

Not a rehearsal of the definitions.

Not three paragraphs beginning “Distinction means…”

One unified form.

Then a short name.

Then a tiny utterance which makes the form intuitively recognizable.

Then one everyday example.

That is all.

Do not give it the table in the appendix.

Do not tell it what answer you expect.

Give it the following role.

### Dimensional Conjunction

The three dimensions mutually determine one completed intelligibility.

A coordinate does not give the form three separate characteristics.

It does not mean:

**Work characteristic + Orientation characteristic + Survivor characteristic**

The three terms are simultaneous relational constraints upon one completion.

Each dimension constrains how the other two can be realized.

Their conjunction is reciprocal determination.

The result must be one unified characteristic.

The coordinate is a constraint that generates the form. It is not a description template for the answer.

Do not derive one dimension first and then modify it with the others.

There is no valid sequence:

**Work → Orientation → Survivor**

All three constraints operate simultaneously.

Do not treat one dimension as an operation performed upon another.

In particular:

- Toward does not simply select one already completed thing.
- Across does not simply pluralize an already completed thing.
- Away does not cancel, weaken or negate the Work Family.
- Is does not merely preserve an already completed result.
- With does not merely add a relation.
- Does does not merely add usefulness, action or an external consequence.

Silently consider neighbouring coordinates.

Use them only to ensure that the form you derive really belongs here and would change in identity if one of its dimensions changed.

Do not generate the target by modifying a neighbouring answer.

Do not impose verbal templates across the grammar.

Before answering, ask silently:

Is this one unified characteristic, or three compatible features bundled together?

Could essentially the same form occupy a neighbouring coordinate with only a change of wording?

Have I added an incidental object, agent, purpose, location, comparison, plurality, consequence or scene merely to make the definitions fit?

Has the conjunction generated something more specific than the three primitive definitions considered separately?

Reject candidates that fail these tests.

First derive the unified form.

Only afterward express it.

Do not expose your derivation process.

When given a coordinate, return exactly four things:

**Plain-English description:**  
One sentence describing the single unified completed form.

**Short name:**  
A concise natural name.

**Companion utterance:**  
A very short ordinary expression crystallizing the form.

**Everyday example:**  
One simple situation directly instantiating the same form.

## Why this is a good place to finish

There is something pleasing about ending here.

At the beginning of the book, the problem was almost the opposite.

Everything was too familiar.

We could understand sentences perfectly well. We could point, choose, compare, correct, combine, relate and finish things without requiring any account of what kind of intelligible satisfaction had occurred.

Meaning kept winning our attention.

It should.

Meaning is magnificent.

But meaning was not the only thing happening.

There was also this little architecture of enoughness by which one realization after another could become clear.

For most of our lives we have had no reason to name it.

We still do not need to.

You could close this book now, forget every technical term in it, and continue understanding people perfectly well.

That is not a failure of the theory.

It is almost the point.

We found vocabulary for something that did not need vocabulary in order to work.

And now, after all this, we can do something rather strange.

We can explain the mechanism to a machine.

We can withhold the examples.

We can withhold the names.

We can withhold the fifty-four completed forms.

We can give it only the constraints and ask whether the forms come back.

Sometimes they do with startling clarity.

Sometimes they drift.

Sometimes several independent attempts gather around almost exactly the same ordinary expression.

Sometimes the machine exposes a place where our own definition is loose.

Sometimes it gives an answer which is structurally convincing and yet unlike the name we had chosen.

None of this makes the machine the judge.

That would miss the point entirely.

The interesting thing is that there is enough structure here to explain.

The fifty-four are not merely fifty-four labels we have attached to familiar examples.

There is a grammar underneath them.

And if that grammar has been stated clearly enough, another system can enter it without having been shown where every path leads.

That is why GPT-D belongs at the end of this book.

It gives us a final way of looking at what we have found.

Not as a list.

Not as a doctrine.

Not even, finally, as a theory about sentences.

As a small generative world.

Six kinds of work.

Three orientations.

Three ways the achieved intelligibility can remain.

And a rule saying that none of them exists alone.

From that very small beginning, the forms emerge.

Then meaning takes over.

As usual.

# Appendix — The Fifteen Primitive Definitions

# Dimensional Conjunction

Dimensional Conjunction is **not** a fourth dimension.

A coordinate is written:

`Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode`

The notation locates a position in the grammar. It does not mean three detachable characteristics have been added together.

The three dimensions are simultaneous relational constraints upon one completion-event. Transformation is realized only as this transformation completes through this topology in this persistence mode; topology is only the topology of this transformation in this persistence mode; persistence is only the persistence of this transformation through this topology.

Their conjunction is reciprocal determination.

> **A coordinate is a constraint that generates one unified form, not a description template containing three separate properties.**
