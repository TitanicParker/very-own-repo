# GPT Episode Append Manual

**Purpose:** Handover and append protocol for any GPT given write access to this repository.

**Status:** Repository instruction. Read this before proposing, drafting, or publishing a new episode.

This is not a style guide in isolation. It is a continuity rule.

The book is cumulative. A new episode is allowed to exist only when it can inherit the reader state created by the book that already exists and leave a further state available for what may come next.

> **Do not append from memory, from a summary, from the latest episode alone, or from the user's proposed topic alone. Read the live reader from the entrance to the living edge first.**

---

# 1. The governing requirement: congruence

A proposed episode must be **congruent with the whole reading experience that precedes it**.

Congruence does not mean that the episode merely discusses the same subject. It means that:

1. the reader has already been given everything the new episode requires;
2. the new episode begins from something genuinely made available by the preceding sequence;
3. it does not silently contradict, reset, rename, or bypass an achieved distinction;
4. it advances the reader into a state that was not yet available before the episode;
5. the episode earns its position in the sequence rather than merely occupying the next number.

The central diagnostic question is:

> **Why can this episode properly appear now, but could not properly have appeared several episodes earlier?**

The answer must be specific. “Because it is about the same theory” is not sufficient.

A good answer names the completed intelligibilities inherited from earlier episodes and shows how they make the new movement possible now.

If that cannot be shown, **do not append the episode**. Explain the continuity problem to the user instead of forcing the material into the sequence.

---

# 2. Mandatory read before every append

Before drafting or writing a new episode to the repository, read the current book **in reader order, from start to finish**.

Do not substitute summaries for this pass.

At minimum, inspect and read:

1. `index.html` — the entrance and introduction;
2. `book.html` — the current public episode order;
3. every live file in `posts/`, in that order, from Episode 1 through the current living edge;
4. `APPENDIX.md` — whenever the proposed episode touches the grammar, the dimensions, the primitive definitions, the 54, coordinates, or structural interpretation;
5. `TESTING_FIELD.md` — whenever the proposed episode cites or analyzes fairy-tale or mathematical testing material.

Also inspect the reader mechanics before publishing:

- `assets/reader-marker.js`
- `service-worker.js`
- `index.html`
- `book.html`

The visible episode list is evidence of the current sequence, but **the sequence must be understood from the episode contents themselves**.

Do not infer continuity from filenames alone.

---

# 3. Reconstruct the reader state

After reading the complete live sequence, write a private continuity note before drafting the next episode.

It should answer four questions:

### A. What does the reader now know?

List only claims and distinctions that the published sequence has actually earned.

### B. What can the reader now feel or recognize without re-teaching it?

This includes recurring intuitions the book has made palpable, not just terminology.

### C. What is newly available at the living edge?

Identify the precise opening created by the final episode. This is the strongest candidate source for the next episode.

### D. What is still unresolved?

Do not invent a problem merely to create another episode. Name only tensions, questions, consequences, tests, or unexplored structures that genuinely remain live after the current ending.

The next episode should arise from the relation between **C** and **D**.

# 5. Semantic invariants that may not be silently broken

These are not optional stylistic preferences. They are part of the reader's already-earned world.

## The finite claim

The 54 are finite forms of completed intelligibility, not 54 meanings, sentence types, topics, rhetorical moves, or kinds of reality.

Meaning may be unbounded. The finite proposal concerns the ways meaning becomes graspable enough.

## No completion, no coordinate

Do not assign a coordinate merely because a sentence exists or because surface grammar resembles a known example.

Recover what actually became complete first.

## Coordinate comes after recovery

Do not begin with a desired cell and reverse-engineer the sentence into it unless the task is explicitly compositional and the resulting completed intelligibility is independently checked.

When interpreting existing language:

`sentence → completed intelligibility → coordinate`

not:

`sentence → keyword/grammar heuristic → cell`

## Dimensional Conjunction is simultaneous

Transformation Pattern × Completion Topology × Persistence Mode are simultaneous relational constraints on one completion-event.

Do not describe them as three serial stages.

## Release means de-governance

Do not infer Release from negation, absence, separation, removal, “away” wording, or physical motion. Something that governed the situation must lose determining force.

## Persistence is not synonymous with Efficacy

Standing, Bearing, and Efficacy are all ways achieved completion can remain available for subsequent uptake.

Do not reduce continuation to Efficacy alone.

## The primitive vocabulary is already complete

The book has told the reader that there are no more primitive pieces coming.

Do not casually introduce a sixteenth primitive, fourth dimension, extra topology, extra persistence mode, or additional cell.

If research genuinely changes the grammar, that is a separate revision task requiring explicit user direction and a deliberate repair of the existing book. Do not smuggle such a change into an appended episode.

## Historical vocabulary is not current public vocabulary

Do not formally revert to Work / Orientation / Survivor or the older value labels in new public prose.

Use the current vocabulary in `APPENDIX.md`.

# 6. What a new episode is allowed to do

A new episode may:

- deepen something already made available;
- test a claim under new pressure;
- reveal a consequence of an earlier distinction;
- move from felt recognition toward greater precision;
- return to the 54 with a question the reader is now equipped to ask;
- use story, proof, ordinary language, physical examples, or other material when the material earns the intended grasp;
- expose an inconsistency or limitation, provided the episode does not conceal it;
- refine the reader's understanding without pretending that refinement was already present earlier;
- create a new opening for a later episode.

A new episode may **not**:

- exist merely because the topic is interesting;
- restart the book's explanation as though the reader has learned nothing;
- require an unintroduced technical distinction and quietly act as though it is established;
- contradict an earlier earned claim without explicitly repairing it;
- enlarge the primitive grammar as a side effect;
- force source sentences into coordinates to create an appearance of coverage;
- turn the book into a catalogue of classifications;
- use the testing field as decorative evidence;
- repeat an earlier episode with different examples but no new reader state;
- jump to a distant implication for which the preceding book has not supplied the bridge;
- append an episode whose real function is to fix an earlier episode. If an earlier episode needs revision, surface that as a revision decision.

# 7. Continuity test for the proposed episode

Before drafting, produce a short **Continuation Warrant** privately.

Use this form:

### Proposed episode
`<working title or subject>`

### Inherited ground
`<what the preceding book has already completed for the reader>`

### Live opening
`<what the current living edge has made newly available>`

### New movement
`<what changes during this episode>`

### Landing
`<what the reader will be able to grasp at the end that was not available at the opening>`

### Why now?
`<why this episode is licensed here rather than several episodes earlier>`

### What becomes available next?
`<the continuation the episode leaves possible without prematurely writing the next episode>`

If any field is vague, especially **Why now?**, the episode is not ready.

# 8. Writing from inside the grammar

From the point at which the 54 have been revealed, the book cannot stand outside the grammar while explaining it.

Every completed intelligibility used in the explanation is itself something that could, in principle, be recovered through the grammar.

This does **not** mean mechanically rotating through cells or labeling every sentence.

For each important sentence or gulp, ask:

> What is this completing for the reader?

> What form of completed intelligibility lets the reader grasp this best?

> Does this sentence leave the right thing available for the next sentence or gulp?

The composition problem is therefore not merely “Is this sentence clear?” It is also:

> **Is this the right completion to inherit from here?**

# 9. Reader-experience invariants

The public object is a serial book made of swipeable gulps.

Use **Episode**, not Chapter, in all newly written public material.

Legacy raw HTML may still contain “Chapter” in places because the shared reader script converts visible labels at runtime. Do not copy that legacy wording into new work. New episodes should be native to the current language.

Reader feel matters:

- one screen should usually carry one intelligible movement;
- gulps may be dense enough to feel substantial, but not so dense that swiping loses its rhythm;
- prose should be intimate, rational, clear, beautiful, and accessible without becoming childish;
- avoid academic throat-clearing;
- avoid management/administrative prose inside the book;
- avoid patronizing instructions;
- do not make the reader memorize the 54;
- examples should earn their place by making a structural phenomenon palpable;
- prefer ordinary, concrete material when it can carry the argument;
- technical precision should arrive after the reader has somewhere for it to land whenever possible.

The interface should remain quiet. The reading, not the controls, is the object.

# 10. Source discipline

When an episode uses material from `TESTING_FIELD.md`, preserve the source wording and order when presenting material as source material.

Do not silently “improve” a fairy-tale sentence or mathematical traversal and then treat the revision as evidence.

When assigning a structural reading to source language, distinguish:

- what the source literally says;
- what completed intelligibility is being recovered;
- what coordinate, if any, follows from that recovery.

Historical analytical notes are not automatically current coordinate assignments.

If a coordinate is uncertain, leave it uncertain until it is earned. Uncertainty is preferable to false precision.

---

# 11. Append, do not rewrite by reflex

The normal operation is **append-only** for public episodes.

Do not revise earlier episodes merely to make the new proposal fit.

Earlier published material may be edited only when:

1. the user explicitly asks for revision; or
2. a genuine contradiction or broken route is discovered and the user authorizes the repair.

If the new episode requires retroactive changes to make its premise true, stop and explain why.

The continuity must be earned by the existing sequence, not manufactured after the fact.

# 12. Mechanical append protocol

Once the episode passes the semantic and reader-experience checks, append it cleanly.

Assume the current living edge is Episode `N` and the new episode is `N+1`.

## Create the episode

Create:

`posts/NNN-<stable-slug>.html`

Follow the established swipeable structure and shared reader behavior.

New visible labels should say `Episode`, not `Chapter`.

The episode's previous-link label should also use `Episode`.

## Update the entrance

Update `index.html` so the episode picker includes the new episode in order.

Do not remove the reader's ability to:

- continue from an automatically saved place;
- browse episodes;
- begin again;
- enter as a visitor;
- install/keep the book when supported.

## Update the contents page

Update `book.html` so the new episode appears at the living edge.

## Update automatic continuity

Update `assets/reader-marker.js`:

- add the new filename to the `episodes` metadata;
- add a `knownNext` link from Episode `N` to Episode `N+1`;
- leave Episode `N+1` without a next mapping until another episode exists.

This is what makes the new episode the new living edge.

Do not break the local reading-position key or discard existing readers' saved positions without a deliberate migration.

## Update the installed reader cache

Update `service-worker.js`:

- add the new episode path to `CORE`;
- increment the cache version whenever the core reader set changes, so installed readers receive the new material rather than remaining on an old precache indefinitely.

## Update repository orientation

Update `README.md` if its reading-order list or description of the living edge is now stale.

Do not create duplicate manifests, duplicate readers, extra working manuscripts, archival copies, provenance files, backup files, or one-shot migration workflows merely to perform the append.

A clean direct commit is preferred.

# 13. Repository hygiene

This repository is intentionally small.

Do not reintroduce the pattern of leaving temporary process artifacts behind.

In particular:

- no `WORKING_2.md`, `FINAL_FINAL.md`, backup copies, or timestamped drafts;
- no superseded episode files left beside the live sequence;
- no provenance/status documents unless the user explicitly asks for them;
- no one-shot GitHub Actions workflow left in `.github/workflows/` after a migration;
- no duplicate canonical grammar file when `APPENDIX.md` is authoritative;
- no duplicate testing-field file when `TESTING_FIELD.md` is authoritative;
- no “archive” directory created merely because something was removed from the live book.

Git history already preserves history. The working tree should express the current commitment.

This manual is hidden under `.gpt/` precisely so it can guide repository agents without becoming part of the reader-facing book.

# 14. Verification after append

Do not call an append complete until all of the following are checked.

### Sequence

- `book.html` lists the episodes in the intended order.
- `index.html` offers the same episode order.
- the former living edge continues into the new episode.
- the new episode ends at `Living edge` rather than pointing to nonexistent material.

### Reader continuity

- exact episode-and-gulp position still saves automatically;
- an existing saved position still resolves correctly;
- “Continue” opens the saved episode and gulp;
- “Begin again” remains available from the entrance;
- visitor mode still permits direct episode selection.

### Installation/offline reader

- `service-worker.js` includes the new episode;
- the cache version was advanced if the core set changed;
- the manifest remains valid;
- no removed file remains in the current cache list.

### Content

- the episode actually passes the Continuation Warrant;
- the opening inherits the preceding book rather than re-teaching it;
- the landing creates a genuine new reader state;
- no primitive definition has drifted from `APPENDIX.md`;
- any quoted or analyzed testing-field material matches `TESTING_FIELD.md`.

### Repository

- no temporary workflow or helper file remains;
- no superseded duplicate was created;
- the public Pages deployment for the final commit succeeds before claiming the live site is current.

# 15. Failure rule

A GPT with repository access is not required to append merely because it has been asked to “write Episode N.”

If the proposed material fails congruence, say why.

If the material is valuable but belongs later, identify what has to become available first.

If the material requires revision of the existing book, distinguish that from an append.

If the theory itself has changed, do not hide the change inside continuity language. Surface the revision explicitly.

The standard is not obedience to the next number.

The standard is lawful continuation.

# 17. The principle behind the protocol

The repository should be built by the same principle the book is describing.

A new episode should not merely come **after** the previous episode.

It should become possible **because of what the previous book has completed**.

And when it completes, it should leave the book with somewhere lawful to go.

> **Append the book the way the book says understanding proceeds: inherit what has become enough, and make the next thing genuinely available.**
